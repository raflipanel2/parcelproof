Sealing a parcel: tape width, H-pattern and common errors

Make a reliable closure with suitable packing tape on clean corrugated board.

A strong carton can still open when the tape is unsuitable, the surface is dusty or the bottom closure was not secured properly. Closure is part of package structure.

ParcelProof EditorialReviewed 11 Nov 2026Independent educational guide

Choose packaging tape, not household shortcuts

Use a tape product suited to corrugated shipping cartons and the shipment’s weight and environment. Masking, gift-wrap and many general household tapes are poor substitutes.

Check the carrier’s guidance, especially for high-weight or specialty boxes. Some packaging methods use different approved tapes or reinforced closures; do not assume one tape type is accepted everywhere.

Understand the main seam and edges

For common regular slotted cartons, an H-pattern uses tape along the central meeting seam and across the two end seams. It can help keep flaps from lifting at corners.

Press tape flat and fully onto clean board. Tape that only touches a torn old adhesive layer has less dependable contact with the carton surface.

Inspect the bottom before packing

It is easy to focus on the top closure while trusting an old bottom seam. Before loading, inspect and seal the bottom with the correct method for the carton and load.

Do not place a dense product into a weak, loosely taped bottom and hope later outer tape will correct the internal pressure problem.

Allow enough overlap and adhesion

Very short tape ends can peel away. Avoid cutting tape exactly at a flap seam where handling can lift the edge. Use a smooth uninterrupted length appropriate for the closure.

Temperature, moisture, dust and old cardboard fibers can change adhesion. Follow tape maker specifications when the environment is challenging.

Inspect after lifting and setting down

Before dispatch, support the package safely and check for lifting flaps or separation near corners. Look at the underside too.

If the carton itself bends or bulges, additional tape may hide the problem without solving it. Repack into a stronger container when necessary.

FIELD EXAMPLE / 14

A reused carton taped only across the middle top seam

The end flaps still lift and the bottom is held by weakened tape. The fix is not a decorative ring of tape around the parcel; inspect the carton strength and replace or reseal all required closure seams with suitable tape.

Decision checkpoint: Tape is a closure system, not a substitute for a strong carton or well-braced contents.

Before you seal the box

  • Choose carton-compatible packing tape.
  • Inspect and secure the bottom seam.
  • Seal the main and end seams appropriately.
  • Apply tape to clean intact surfaces.
  • Check for lifted corners before dispatch.

Common questions

Can duct tape replace packaging tape?

Do not assume it will. Follow the carrier’s specified acceptable closure materials.

Does wrapping more tape around a box make it stronger?

Not necessarily. Excess tape cannot restore crushed box walls or fix a shifting heavy object.

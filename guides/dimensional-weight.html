Field guide 15 / Costs & rules

Dimensional weight explained with a worked example

Learn why large lightweight parcels may be billed by volume, not just by a scale reading.

Shipping rates can reflect both how heavy a package is and how much transport space it occupies. Dimensional weight is one method carriers use to account for that space.

ParcelProof EditorialReviewed 11 Nov 2026Independent educational guide

Measure the outer packed parcel

Use length, width and height of the completed shipping container, not the retail product or its internal cavity. Carriers may specify rounding each dimension before the calculation.

For irregular forms, carriers may use a bounding rectangle or other rules. Do not assume packing a pillow in a bulging poly mailer removes volume-based billing.

Calculate with the correct divisor

The basic formula is length × width × height divided by the applicable dimensional factor. Use centimeters with a cubic-centimeter-per-kilogram divisor, or inches with the corresponding cubic-inch-per-pound factor—never mix the units.

The divisor can vary by service, account, country and rate plan. The value prefilled in our tool is an example only; verify the actual one from the current carrier service.

Compare with actual scale weight

The carrier may compare dimensional and actual weight and rate on the larger figure, with a service-specific minimum or rounding rule. This is the idea of chargeable or billable weight.

Actual weight must include box, tape, inserts and all contents. A scale reading for the bare product is incomplete and can lead to an adjustment after shipping.

Watch shape and adjustment fees

Extra-large or irregular parcels may incur additional handling, dimension or oversize charges separate from the weight comparison. The base weight formula is not a full rate quote.

A small size mismeasurement can matter when a threshold or increment is crossed. Measure after sealing rather than trusting supplier dimensions blindly.

Reduce waste without underpacking

Choosing a more proportional box can reduce empty space and potentially dimensional weight. But removing necessary cushioning to hit a price band may create a more expensive damage problem.

Compare several safe packaging options and any carrier packaging specification before settling on the smallest dimension. Our calculator lets you model those assumptions.

A 60 × 45 × 35 cm box weighing 6 kg

Its volume is 94,500 cm³. With an illustrative divisor of 5,000 cm³/kg, the raw dimensional weight is 18.9 kg—much higher than its 6 kg scale weight. The final rating depends on the applicable rounding rule, contract and additional fees.

Before you seal the box

  • Measure the closed outside dimensions.
  • Weigh the complete parcel.
  • Check the actual service divisor.
  • Apply the correct rounding rules.
  • Ask about surcharges separately.

Common questions

Is 5000 always the divisor?

No. It is a familiar metric example, but services and contracts vary.

Will my charge equal DIM weight times a fixed price?

Not necessarily. Zone, service, discounts, size thresholds, fees and other factors can affect the final quote.

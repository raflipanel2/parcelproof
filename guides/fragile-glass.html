Field guide 03 / Fragile

Packing glass objects without relying on “Fragile” stickers

A method for protecting thin edges, stems and hollow glassware when using a parcel carrier.

Glass often fails at a specific weak location long before its surrounding box looks damaged. The aim is to keep impact energy away from rims, stems, corners and neighboring pieces—not to count how many fragile labels are attached.

ParcelProof EditorialReviewed 11 Nov 2026Independent educational guide

Start with the weakest feature

Inspect the glass for existing chips and cracks. Note stems, handles, spouts and thin rims that could concentrate a force. Pack each item separately rather than allowing a pair of glasses to knock against one another.

For a hollow vessel, add appropriate support inside only if doing so will not stress the walls. Never press rigid material into fragile thin glass. Stabilize stems or delicate components with fitted protective structures.

Create a complete protected item

Begin with a clean soft surface layer if the finish could scratch. Add an impact-absorbing wrap or fitted supports, paying special attention to edges and exposed projections. The purpose is not decoration; the item should become an independent protected unit.

A long slender glass object may benefit from a purpose-designed insert or inner box rather than an unstructured wrap. Keep wrapped objects from carrying the weight of a much heavier item above them.

Center the protected glass inside the carton

The packed glass should not touch the bottom, sides, top or corners of the outer shipping box. Put the lower cushion in before lowering the item, rather than sliding filler around it afterward.

For very delicate goods, double-boxing can increase separation from outer handling impacts. FedEx describes double-box methods for breakables, but any exact amount of cushioning must be verified against the relevant service and item.

Secure the assembly against sliding

Fill remaining spaces with material that holds the protected unit stable. Loose fill may settle, so do not depend on it alone to keep a heavy or dense piece suspended. Recheck top coverage after closing the flaps.

Do not overtighten packaging against a brittle rim. A parcel can fail because the packaging itself imposes a concentrated load, even when the box is intact.

Mark clearly but rely on construction

Use clear label placement, secure seams and any carrier-requested handling marks. “Fragile” can communicate a request; it does not guarantee manual handling or change how automated processing systems work.

Photograph the condition and completed pack before dispatch if you need a record. Keep receipts and any carrier packaging requirements relevant to reimbursement or claims.

FIELD EXAMPLE / 03

A handblown glass vase with a narrow neck

The vase has a thick base but a vulnerable narrow neck. A simple bubble-wrap sleeve around the body could leave the neck exposed. A shaped neck support, separated outer cushion and stable upright packing unit are more useful than adding a second fragile sticker.

Decision checkpoint: If the item is one-of-a-kind, very fragile or high value, ask about specialist packaging and the carrier’s acceptance requirements before mailing.

Before you seal the box

  • Inspect rims, necks and handles.
  • Wrap fragile items individually.
  • Keep the protected unit off all box walls.
  • Prevent shifts without squeezing the glass.
  • Photograph the finished protective pack.

Common questions

Does a fragile sticker prevent breakage?

No. Packaging is still expected to withstand ordinary handling and sorting.

Can glass pieces share a box?

They can only when individually protected and separated so they cannot strike or load against one another.

Field guide 18 / Shipping prep

If a package arrives damaged: what to document

Create a useful record, preserve packaging, and understand why claims depend on service terms.

A dented outer box does not always mean the item is damaged; a pristine box does not guarantee the contents are intact. A clear record can help the recipient and sender identify what happened.

ParcelProof EditorialReviewed 11 Nov 2026Independent educational guide

Separate exterior damage from item damage

Photograph the parcel before opening if safe and practical, including corners, seams and the shipping label. Make a note of wetness, punctures or crushing.

After opening, document the protective material and item condition before discarding packaging. Avoid changing the scene simply to make a stronger-looking claim.

Preserve the packing components

Keep the outer carton, inserts, wraps and dividers until the relevant claim or return process allows disposal. The carrier may request inspection or photographs of original packaging.

A replacement box used after the delivery may no longer show the initial packing method. Record what was present rather than reconstructing a new version.

Check the correct claim route

The buyer, seller, sender and carrier may have different rights or responsibilities depending on the transaction and service. Check the actual agreement instead of assuming anyone can file any claim.

A declared value, shipping insurance product and refund policy are not always interchangeable. Read the relevant terms and submission deadlines.

Write a factual timeline

Keep dates, acceptance scans, delivery information, communication history and the observed damage in one place. Avoid speculation about the exact moment or cause unless documented.

For a business shipment, coordinate with the recipient so that inspection and preservation instructions are followed before items are discarded or repaired.

Use the lesson to improve future packs

If multiple similar items are damaged, look for a repeatable failure point: inadequate bottom cushion, touching items, weak carton, heavy accessory or moisture exposure.

Do not infer that one shipment proves a packaging method is universally adequate or defective. Document patterns and adapt the pack using reliable carrier guidance.

FIELD EXAMPLE / 18

A framed photo arrives with a crushed lower corner

The recipient photographs the closed parcel, then the inner corner protectors and cracked frame, retaining the box. A factual report names the damage and packaging present. It does not claim a specific sorting-machine failure without evidence.

Decision checkpoint: Preserve real evidence and follow the claims process for the actual transaction and carrier, including any time limits.

Before you seal the box

  • Photograph the package before unpacking.
  • Document inner supports and item condition.
  • Keep all packaging temporarily.
  • Check service claim requirements and deadlines.
  • Record observed facts rather than guesses.

Common questions

Should I throw away a visibly crushed carton?

Keep it until the responsible carrier or seller explains whether inspection or evidence is needed.

Does declared value guarantee full reimbursement?

No. Coverage terms, exclusions, evidence and claim procedures vary by service and product.

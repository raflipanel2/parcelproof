Field guide 04 / Fragile

Shipping mugs, bowls and ceramics without chipped edges

Treat handles, hollow forms and glaze as distinct risks rather than packing every ceramic piece identically.

Ceramic pieces are strong in some directions and surprisingly fragile in others. A handle, spout, raised foot ring or exposed rim can fracture during an ordinary handling shock. The packing sequence should protect those details first.

ParcelProof EditorialReviewed 11 Nov 2026Independent educational guide

Inspect and document condition

Look carefully at the rim, glaze and handle junction before packing. Existing hairline damage can become worse in transit. Photograph visible defects and describe them accurately if arranging a sale or return.

Do not assume a heavy ceramic item is robust. Weight can make an impact more destructive when a mug shifts into the outer carton or another packed object.

Support projections without pulling on them

A protruding handle needs a protective pocket or fitted surround, not a tightly wrapped loop that makes the handle carry the force of the whole object. Avoid taping directly to a glaze or decorative surface.

For hollow pieces, carefully chosen soft filler may reduce pressure on vulnerable cavities, but it should not exert force against a thin lip. Separate the object from any hard decorative lid or accessory.

Choose individual compartments for multiples

Stacking cups directly is risky even when each cup seems wrapped. A divider or nested protective unit can prevent a rim from contacting another piece during vibration.

If a set includes a heavy teapot and delicate cups, do not let the weight of the teapot rest on the cups. Use a strong partitioned box or split into separate parcels where appropriate.

Put cushion under the weight-bearing base

A mug or bowl often lands base-first. Place bottom cushioning before the item, and prevent the foot ring from cutting through the padding. Add lateral and top buffers to complete the six-sided system.

Test movement after sealing temporarily. If the package is top-heavy or creates a hard thump when turned, reopen and revise the internal supports.

Consider the value and replaceability

Handmade or collectible ceramics can justify a purpose-built pack, double box or specialist service. Compare not just postage but the cost of a damaged irreplaceable piece.

Ask the carrier about packaging criteria and claims evidence. Insurance terminology and reimbursement may differ substantially between products and services.

FIELD EXAMPLE / 04

Two mugs from a handmade pottery studio

One mug has a delicate loop handle and the other a thick handle. Identical wrapping does not provide identical protection. Make a stable support for each handle, separate the mugs with a rigid divider, and keep both away from the carton’s exterior.

Decision checkpoint: Protect projecting features independently, then stabilize the whole ceramic unit; avoid loading delicate handles with adjacent objects.

Before you seal the box

  • Check glaze and rim condition.
  • Protect handles and foot rings.
  • Use dividers between pieces.
  • Cushion beneath heavy bases.
  • Record packaging when value is significant.

Common questions

Is newspaper safe directly on the glaze?

Ink and abrasion can be a problem for some surfaces; use a clean nonabrasive inner layer first.

Is double-boxing always required?

Not for every object. It is an option for higher-risk goods, subject to item and carrier requirements.

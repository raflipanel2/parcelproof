Packing dense and heavy objects without bottom-box failure

Plan concentrated loads, strong supports and safe lifting before shipping weighty parcels.

Heavy goods can be deceptively compact. Their most important risks include bottom seam failure, sharp concentrated pressure and dangerous lifting—not just bruised corners.

ParcelProof EditorialReviewed 11 Nov 2026Independent educational guide

Start with the parcel’s total weight

Weigh the product, its accessories, cushioning and outer carton together. Check that the chosen carrier service and packaging are approved for the fully packed gross weight.

Do not use weight limits from another carrier or another country as though they apply universally. Large or heavy parcels may require special labeling or freight handling.

Distribute the contact load

A metal machine foot can push through cardboard even when the total weight seems acceptable. Use strong internal load-spreading structures so weight does not concentrate on a small area.

Dense items may require heavy-duty foam, blocks, rigid pads or other engineered bracing rather than lightweight crumpled paper. Keep supports from damaging delicate surfaces.

Protect the bottom closure

The bottom is a common stress point when a carton is lifted or placed down. Use the appropriate carton grade and carrier-accepted reinforced sealing method.

Do not assume a weak box becomes strong merely because many strips of tape have been applied. Damaged corrugated walls can fail independently of the closure.

Immobilize heavy components

A heavy object that slides a few centimeters can strike a wall with considerable force. Block movement in all directions and secure any detachable weighty accessories separately.

If the item has casters, handles or exposed legs, protect them as load-concentration points. Some products should be palletized or shipped through a different service.

Design for safe lifting and transfer

Consider whether an individual can lift the completed parcel without injury. Check service size limits, handling surcharges and any required heavy-package marks.

If the pack cannot be safely handled, investigate a freight or professional packing option rather than forcing the shipment into an ordinary small-package workflow.

FIELD EXAMPLE / 11

A compact cast-iron part in a large carton

A 15 kg metal component sits on a 2 cm layer of crumpled paper. The paper can compress, leaving a hard contact point on the bottom panel. A strong carton with rigid load-spreading supports and positive blocking is more appropriate.

Decision checkpoint: Solve concentrated loading and internal movement before choosing tape; check the correct carrier’s current heavy-package rules.

Before you seal the box

  • Weigh the fully packed parcel.
  • Inspect carton gross-weight rating.
  • Spread load away from narrow feet or edges.
  • Brace all heavy moving components.
  • Assess lifting and service limits.

Common questions

Can I use regular packing peanuts for a dense machine part?

Loose lightweight filler may compress or shift and may be unsuitable as the primary support for a dense item.

Is a heavy-item label enough?

No. Safe package construction and accepted service limits matter more than the sticker.
